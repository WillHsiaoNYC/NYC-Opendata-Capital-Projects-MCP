02

Recommendations for this year

Prompt · User

based on those narrative fields of each project, what would you recommend a new list of typologies for this year? and why?

Answer · Codex

I recommend classifying projects by the asset or facility being changed, with separate tags for the work performed. That would make this year’s typologies more consistent and more useful for comparing budgets and schedules.

I checked the existing narrative evidence and the MCP’s May 2026 snapshot, covering 3,103 distinct schedule PIDs. The list below is a proposed scheme, not a completed reclassification.

Why I would change last year’s list

  • Separate agency ownership from facility function. “EDC-Managed Facility” can describe very different buildings. Arthur Avenue Market’s narrative supports a market-building typology. Likewise, a maintenance shop should remain an operations building even when its plan category says “Child Welfare Facilities.”
  • Divide the broad Parks categories. A recreation center, restroom building, playground and forest-restoration project have different physical scope. The narratives support those distinctions.
  • Make geography a separate attribute. Replace “Upstate Water Supply” and “Downstate Wastewater Treatment” with functional labels. PID 4756 explicitly describes equipment replacement at upstate wastewater-treatment plants.
  • Separate green stormwater from CSO facilities. Newtown Creek’s CSO storage tunnel and Flushing Creek’s Bluebelt wetland represent different assets and construction approaches. Record a consent-decree requirement separately when documented.
  • Determine bridge use from the narrative. PID 2058 describes pedestrian bridges over Belt Parkway. Last year’s rule assigning DOT-sponsored bridges to “Vehicular Bridges” would mislabel that project.
  • Merge overlapping health labels. “Health Services Facility” and “Healthcare Facility” become one family, with hospital, clinic and laboratory subtypes.

I would also record work-type tags: new construction/expansion, rehabilitation, resurfacing, roof/façade, building systems, accessibility, demolition, and site work. Use additional purpose tags for resilience, energy efficiency and environmental compliance. For example, library HVAC replacement becomes Libraries → building systems → energy efficiency, when the text supports each tag.

For coding, give explicit project scope the most weight, examine all linked FMS descriptions, and use plan categories as supporting evidence. Preserve secondary asset tags for combined projects and an unresolved status when the text cannot establish a primary typology. The City also provides an agency project description field, which I recommend adding to this year’s review because it can explain scope that short names omit. NYC source and field definitions

This structure would let the class compare both what kinds of assets are being improved and what kinds of work are driving cost or schedule differences.
